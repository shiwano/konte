# Lineup and boundaries

- **`lineup` lists a narrative frame's characters left to right** — include a sleeve or shoulder visible at the edge; use `[]` for an empty frame or prop insert.
- **A prop is never lined up** — the row has one axis and a prop sits in front of or behind as often as beside. Write where it is relative to whoever holds it or set it down, in the prompt.
- **Write the frame, not the roster** — a push-in onto one person lists that person. konte remembers the continuity itself, accumulating "a is left of b" per location, and a later shot that contradicts an earlier one is `lineup-flipped`.
- **`lineupTo` is the frame the shot LEAVES, in full** — declare it wherever the frame changes inside the shot: a move, an entrance, an exit, or a change meant to land on the next cut. Symmetrical with `lineup`, so list everyone the closing frame holds; whoever you leave out has walked out of it, and their place stops being known until a later frame holds them again. The shots after it read against it. Identical to the `lineup` is `lineup-vacuous` — leave it out.
- **A camera coming round reverses everyone, so list everyone** — half a frame re-declared reads as the rest walking out of shot, and nothing will catch it.
- **A frame holds whoever it spans** — two people with a third between them cannot be framed together without that third in shot, sleeve or shoulder (`lineup-gap`). Either list them, or the shot is on a frame that does not hold both ends.
- **`join` is what the boundary INTO this shot is** — leave it out for an ordinary cut with story time running on; `"jump-back"` enters a flashback and `"jump-forward"` skips ahead or leaves one, and both empty the accumulated order everywhere (given time, people move). `"continuous"` says there is no cut at all: this shot and the one before it are one unbroken take.
- **Where the shot before is on the same `setup`, `join` is required** — `"continuous"` for a long take, `"jump-forward"` for a jump cut or a gap, `"jump-back"` for a flashback. `"continuous"` is refused anywhere else — a different setup, an aside or graphic shot before it, or the opening shot.
- **A long take is one camera position** — a hold whose camera moves is one shot with two `<Panel>`s. Two `"continuous"` shots must also agree on the frame between them: the first one's `lineupTo` (or its `lineup`) is the second one's `lineup`, and the video has to pin that one frame.
- **`cutin` is a second camera frame over the shot** — `{ setup, lineup, lineupTo?, join? }` on a narrative or graphic shot (the presenter wiped in over a screen). It is one more shot on that setup: every lineup, plate and keyframe rule applies, findings keyed `<shotId>.cutin`. `cutin.join` is the wipe's own boundary, on the same terms against the cutin over the shot before. Placement is the composition's; an inset no camera takes (a phone screen) needs no `cutin`.
