# A piece without a story

## The skeleton is the song's sections or the lens's roles

- **Cut to a song → its sections are the acts** — intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro; one visual idea per section, the strongest on the last chorus. Map section → what the picture does there before writing any shot.
- **No song → the lens's roles** — `mood-piece`: atmosphere → motif → variation → **peak** → fade.
- **Test: delete a section's shots** — if no motif is dropped and no step of the build goes missing, the section has no idea yet.

## A motif carries it

- **One or two motifs — an object, a gesture, a light, a color** — "the neon sign stutters on", never "urban loneliness"; planted in the first section, returned to in every later one.

## Build by repetition and intensification

- **A return never comes back unchanged** — the second chorus raises scale, speed, light, the number of figures, or the camera's distance; the same frame with one thing changed reads as a return, an unchanged copy as a loop.
- **The peak is the motif at its most** — the last chorus or the lens's `peak`: the most distinct frame and the fastest cutting. Spend nothing bigger before it.
- **A quiet section before the peak** — a bridge, a held frame; the peak is measured against it.
- **Cut rhythm follows the song's energy** — longer shots in a verse, shorter in a chorus.

## Pitching

- **Pitch a section map, not a chain of events** — one line each: the world, the motif, how the peak lands.
- **Never slip an incident in** — a pitch with an event nobody asked for is the first one cut.
- **A music video's first fork is the performer** — on screen singing, dancing, never shown; ask it, since a mouth to match decides the production (`production-guide` step 2).
