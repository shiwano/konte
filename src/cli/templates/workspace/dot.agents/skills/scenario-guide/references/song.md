# A piece cut to a song

## Write and revise lyrics

- Set the song's core before drafting — whose words, to whom, and what they want to say; for a chant or dance song, the attitude and sound can carry the core. Use the agreed language and tone.
- Draft three distinct hook phrases, then choose one internally — vary the thought or sound, keep the one that carries the core and invites repetition, then build the sections around it.
- Give the singer a way of seeing — choose words, observations and details they would notice; let the lyric add something beyond describing the picture.
- Make each section earn its place — develop the thought, shift the perspective or sustain a deliberate groove; keep a repeated hook recognizable.
- Before review, revise the draft line by line — find interchangeable filler, picture-only narration, unclear referents, and meaning or natural phrasing bent to fit a rhyme. Rewrite weak lines against the song's core.
- Speak or sing the lines over the intended pulse — check stress, breath, crowded syllables and the hook's repeatability; revise awkward phrases before generation.
- Judge against the chosen style — familiar rhymes, abstraction and simple repetition can work; keep them when their meaning or sound earns the place. No required confession, concrete-object quota or banned-word list.

## Shape the picture

- The song's sections are the time skeleton; the form fills it — map each section to a lens role before writing any shot: a story's turn on the bridge and its payoff on the last chorus, a mood's peak on the last chorus.
- One visual idea per section, the strongest on the last chorus.
- Cut rhythm follows the song's energy — longer shots in a verse, shorter in a chorus.
- The first fork is the performer — on screen singing, dancing, or never shown; ask it — a mouth to match decides the production (`production-guide` step 2).
- Pitch a section map — one line per section: what the picture does there.
